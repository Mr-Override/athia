About Athia

An independent, open-source operating system being built from the ground up in Rust.

The Origin

Athia was born from a simple observation: most operating systems carry decades of legacy decisions, compromises, and accumulated complexity. They were designed for a different era, with different assumptions about security, privacy, and user control.

Athia is different. It is being built from scratch, with modern principles at its core. No legacy code. No inherited compromises. Just a clean slate and a clear vision.

The project is written entirely in Rust, a language chosen for its memory safety guarantees and modern concurrency model. The architecture is a microkernel design, where only the absolute minimum runs in kernel space, and everything else — filesystems, device drivers, networking — runs as isolated user-space services.

The Vision

"An operating system that respects your intelligence, protects your privacy, and gives you complete control over your machine — without sacrificing usability or beauty."

Nine Founding Principles

These principles guide every design and implementation decision in the Athia project.

User Sovereignty

You own your computer. No corporation, no cloud — you. Your data, your decisions, your machine.

Security First

Security is built in from the ground up, not bolted on later. Every design decision considers the security implications.

Open Source

Everything is transparent. Everything is discussable. The code is open for anyone to inspect, modify, and improve.

Offline First

Athia is fully functional without an internet connection. No phone home, no telemetry, no hidden dependencies.

Transparency

Every line of code is visible and open to review. Decisions are documented and publicly discussed.

Community Ownership

Built collaboratively, governed transparently. The community shapes the direction of the project.

Simplicity

Simple systems are secure systems. Complexity is the enemy of reliability and understandability.

Purposeful Design

Every feature exists for a reason. No bloat, no legacy cruft — just intentional, well-designed software.

Sustainable Growth

Quality over speed. We build for the long term, not for hype cycles or artificial deadlines.

What Athia Is

  • An independent operating system built from scratch in Rust
  • A microkernel-based system with capability-based security
  • An open-source project under GPL-3.0-or-later
  • A community-driven project with transparent governance
  • A platform designed for user sovereignty and privacy
  • A complete OS with its own desktop environment and application framework

What Athia Is Not

  • A Linux distribution or kernel module
  • A fork of any existing operating system
  • A hobby project — it has a clear roadmap and governance
  • Dependent on any cloud service or online account
  • A replacement for your current OS yet — it is in early development
  • Closed source or proprietary in any way

The Manifesto

The Athia Manifesto articulates the core beliefs that drive the project. It is a statement of intent — not just about what we are building, but why we are building it.

"We believe that the computer you hold in your hands or sit in front of should be yours — completely and unconditionally. Not leased from a cloud, not managed by a corporation, not constrained by artificial limitations."

"We believe that security should be a default, not an afterthought. That privacy should be guaranteed, not negotiated. That software should be transparent, not opaque."

"We believe that building an operating system from scratch is not just possible — it is necessary. The problems we face today did not exist at the beginning. They were added, layer by layer, compromise by compromise. We can do better."

Read the full manifesto in the Athia repository.

Current Status

Athia is currently in the pre-development phase. The documentation, architecture design, and project structure are in place, but no functional OS code has been implemented yet. The roadmap outlines the planned development phases, from kernel foundation to a stable, daily-driver-quality operating system.
