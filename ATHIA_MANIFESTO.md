# The Athia Manifesto

## Your machine. Your rules.

Computers are personal. They sit on our desks, rest in our laps, and live in our pockets. They hold our memories, our work, our communications, and our creative output. And yet, for too long, the people who use these machines have had diminishing say over how they work.

We believe this is wrong.

## Why Athia Exists

The modern computing landscape has drifted away from user control. Operating systems have become platforms for data collection, mandatory update cycles, and cloud dependency. Features are locked behind accounts. Functionality requires internet connectivity. Users are treated as products rather than owners.

Athia exists to reverse this trend.

We are building an operating system from the ground up — not by forking, customizing, or extending an existing one, but by starting fresh with modern tools, modern principles, and a modern understanding of what users need.

## Our Beliefs

**You own your computer.** Not a corporation, not a cloud provider, not a community of developers. You. The person who turns it on, the person who uses it, the person who depends on it. An operating system should serve its user, not the other way around.

**Security is a right, not a feature.** Security cannot be an add-on, a setting, or a premium tier. It must be woven into every layer of the system, from the kernel up. Capability-based access, process isolation, and sandboxing are not optional — they are the foundation.

**Transparency builds trust.** Every line of code, every design decision, every architectural choice should be visible, discussable, and challengeable. Open source is not just a licensing model — it is a commitment to honest development.

**The internet is a tool, not a requirement.** Your operating system should work when you are offline. It should not phone home. It should not require activation. It should not degrade when disconnected. Connectivity is a convenience, not a prerequisite.

**Updates are yours to control.** No silent forced restarts. No unexpected behavior changes. No features removed without consent. When an update is available, you decide when — and whether — to apply it.

**Privacy is the default.** No mandatory accounts. No telemetry. No analytics. No data collection. If the system needs to send data somewhere, you should know about it, understand why, and have the ability to say no.

**Your environment should reflect you.** From the desktop layout to the window management style, from color themes to animation preferences — every aspect of the experience should be customizable. The system should adapt to you, not the reverse.

**Software should last.** Operating systems should not become obsolete on a schedule. Users should be able to maintain, update, and extend their systems for as long as they choose. Longevity is a feature.

**Community ownership matters.** No single entity should control the direction of a tool that so many people depend on. Governance should be transparent, inclusive, and accountable to the community that builds and uses the software.

## What We Are Building

Athia is an independent operating system written primarily in Rust. It has its own microkernel, its own system services, its own desktop environment, and its own application framework. It does not depend on Linux, BSD, or any other existing operating system.

We chose Rust because it provides memory safety without sacrificing performance. We chose a microkernel because it provides isolation without sacrificing modularity. We chose open source because transparency is non-negotiable.

## What We Are Not

Athia is not a hobby project. We are building a complete, polished, secure, and fully usable operating system. The bar is high, and we intend to meet it.

Athia is not a replacement for every operating system in every scenario. Different tools serve different needs. Athia aims to serve the people who value control, security, and transparency — and who are willing to build those values into reality.

Athia is not finished. It is not even close. But the direction is clear, the principles are firm, and the community is growing.

## An Invitation

Building an operating system is one of the most ambitious software projects possible. It requires kernel engineers, systems programmers, UI designers, documentation writers, testers, security researchers, and community organizers.

If you believe that users should own their computers, that security should be foundational, that software should be transparent, and that the best tools are built by the people who use them — we want you to join us.

You don't need to be an expert. You need to care about the problem, respect the process, and be willing to contribute where you can.

## A Promise

We promise to build Athia with integrity. We will not cut corners on security. We will not compromise on transparency. We will not force updates on our users. We will not collect data without consent. We will not abandon the principles that brought us here.

Built for the people who use it. Owned by the people who build it. Controlled by the people who run it.

**Your machine. Your rules.**
